"Hard sciences" and "soft sciences" is a phrase doing more work than its grammar suggests. It looks like a description of methodology. It functions as a ranking. Whatever the speaker claims to mean by it, the effect is the same: one side gets prestige, the other gets to defend its existence.

The binary collapses on inspection. "Hardness" is a portmanteau of at least five things — degree of mathematization, experimental controllability, predictive precision, institutional funding, and whether the object of study reads papers about itself — none of which co-vary cleanly. Physics scores high on most. Cosmology can't run experiments on the universe and still gets to be hard. Anthropology, taking the soft slot by default, can be ferociously rigorous in some domains — kinship analysis, phonology, ethnographic verification — and would still be called soft by the funding council. The honest picture is multidimensional. The binary survives because it's useful to someone.

What "soft" actually inherits is a 19th-century semantics. Comte put sociology at the bottom of his hierarchy of sciences and physics at the top, on a developmental ladder from theological to metaphysical to positive. The German tradition pushed back — Dilthey's *Geisteswissenschaften*, *Verstehen* as a different mode of knowing — but English-language discourse mostly absorbed the rank order. "Soft" also carries a gendered residue (pliable, mushy, effeminate) and the disciplines coded soft historically admitted women earlier and in greater numbers. The word does work the description can't take credit for.

---

Here is the cleanest definition I can offer of the thing the binary keeps mis-naming.

Science is a specific kind of *relationship to error*. Not a body of facts, not a method (methods vary wildly between physics, paleontology, ecology, epidemiology), not an institution. It is an activity defined by maintaining a working corrective channel — a way for the world to push back on the model, and a willingness to be pushed. This is the same shape [[journal:lo-que-corrige-el-mapa#:~:text=the%20deep%20thing%20about%20knowing|the channel argument]] takes in the small, applied one level up to traditions of inquiry. Anything that has a working corrective channel and uses it is doing science in the sense that matters. Anything without it isn't, no matter how many equations or peer reviews are stacked on top. ^relationship-to-error

This collapses some distinctions and lights up others. String theory has the equations and a wobbly corrective channel — physicists worry about this in print. Polynesian wayfinding has no equations and a brutal corrective channel: you arrive at the island or you don't. Pacific Northwest fire ecology, salmon-aware burning, is corrected every season by whether the salmon return. The work of an *alfarera* — generations of clay, water, glaze, kiln, firing — is corrected by whether the pot survives the heat. The kiln doesn't care about peer review. The pot either holds water or it doesn't — [[journal:el-lazo-que-cierra-los-lazos#^stakes-that-don-t-care|something that continues regardless of whether you noticed]]. ^kiln-as-channel

Macroeconomics, by contrast, has the credentials and a forecasting record so bad that "corrective channel" might be generous. The hardness of an inquiry tracks how well the channel works, not how mathematical the outputs look.

---

This makes the question "is X really science?" look different. It looks, in fact, badly posed.

The word "science" is being used at two levels of abstraction without anyone marking the shift. There is *Science₁*: the general class of systematic empirical practices — observation, hypothesis, intergenerational testing, error-correction. And there is *Science₂*: one particular historical instance of that class — Western post-Enlightenment institutional science, with its specific norms (peer review, written formalism, decontextualized universal claims, professional credentialing).

When someone asks "is indigenous knowledge really science?", they are nominally testing membership in Science₁. The criteria they apply are Science₂'s. The historical instance is impersonating the general class. Or, from the other direction: Science₂ adjudicates membership in Science₁ while sharing its name with the class it sits inside. ^two-sciences

This isn't strictly Russell's paradox, but it has the same self-reference structure — the member marking its own homework about the class it belongs to. The question only sounds reasonable because the levels have been silently collapsed. The wayfinder is doing Science₁. The *alfarera* is doing Science₁. The empirical pharmacology embedded in eight hundred years of *yerbatera* practice is Science₁. None of it is Science₂, and the question of whether any of it "counts" is the wrong question. It's a category error performed as a credentialing decision.

There is also a level confusion underneath the category one. *Hard vs. soft* presents itself as a descriptive claim about methodology. It operates as a metamessage about what counts as knowledge. The content says *this is what science is*; the context says *this is what we will fund, hire, cite, take seriously*. The hierarchy does work the description disavows. That's why arguing with the description has been so unproductive for a century — the metamessage doesn't move when the message gets refuted.

---

Comte built the hierarchy. Tylor mapped its cultural analogue — savagery to barbarism to civilization, animism to monotheism, the West at the apex. Morgan made the kinship and the schema both. The 19th-century positivists wrote down the shape of the assumption in plain terms and named the bottom slots after the things they wanted to keep there.

The intellectual argument against them is over. They lost. Kuhn took the ahistoricism, Feyerabend took the methodism, Latour took the social-construction blindness, postcolonial science studies took the rest. *Primitive Culture* is on the syllabus now as the cautionary text. Comte's hierarchy is in every introductory philosophy-of-science course as the thing to argue against.

The institutional argument is not over. They won that one.

The NSF-to-NEH budget ratio is Comte's hierarchy in dollar form. The IRB framework is designed for the kind of research subject Tylor was studying *as*. Development economics talks about "developing countries" — the word *is* the schema. Indigenous knowledge gets indexed and tabulated under "traditional ecological knowledge" — a category whose existence presumes a non-traditional, non-ecological knowledge that gets to do the indexing.

The discipline teaches Tylor as a cautionary tale inside structures Comte designed. The professed message is *we have moved past this*; the structural message is *this hierarchy still pays the bills*. The shape repeats at every scale where institutional infrastructure outlives the ideas it was built around — [[journal:al-borde-del-fenomeno#^global-south-channels|the same paving move]] under a different name, this time inside the academy rather than across geographies. ^furniture-rearranged

You cannot update one level without the other and call it learning. You have rearranged the furniture in the room you were trying to leave.

---

There is a temptation I have been writing toward and want to name before it eats this entry: to argue that excluded knowledge traditions are *really* doing science, and therefore deserve recognition. The gesture sounds inclusive. It is also the gesture by which Science₂ stays the adjudicator. *We will let you into the club. The club is the thing.* ^inclusion-as-structure

The *alfarera* doesn't need the club. The wayfinder doesn't need the club. The pharmacology that produced the cures the lab is still cataloguing doesn't need the club. The club needs them, to keep the surface story of universal validity intact while its actual edges narrow. ^practice-survives-empire

What I can do, working inside the institution, is name the levels. Mark when Science₁ is being asked and Science₂ is doing the asking. Be honest that my standing to say any of this comes from credentials Science₂ issued, which is the joke and also the constraint. The corrective channel for this writing, for me, runs through people whose practice was here before the demarcation question existed, and will still be here after. What [[journal:lo-que-el-espejo-no-corrige#^cost-of-refusal|cannot refuse]] cannot correct. ^refusal-is-correction The institution that defines membership in the category cannot be refused by what it gatekeeps — which means the institution, by its own definition, cannot be corrected by them. The correction has to come from somewhere else.

That's where this entry stops being a polemic and starts being something I have to go ask about.
