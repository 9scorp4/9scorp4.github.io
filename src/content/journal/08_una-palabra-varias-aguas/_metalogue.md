NAVEGANTE: ¿Sabes si la imprenta sigue abriendo a las seis?

I: I don't know. I haven't been by in months.

NAVEGANTE: No importa. I'll walk by and see.

(NAVEGANTE takes a folded paper from inside their jacket, consults it briefly, refolds it, returns it to the jacket.)

NAVEGANTE: You're writing.

I: Yes.

NAVEGANTE: What.

I: A — kind of a journal. I'm trying to think about a word.

NAVEGANTE: One word.

I: One word.

NAVEGANTE: Which word.

I: *Multipolaridad.*

NAVEGANTE: Ah.

---

NAVEGANTE: ¿Y qué dice tu cuaderno sobre la palabra?

I: That it does different work depending on who uses it. That from one position it means a new configuration of powers, and from another it means more partners to deal with, and that these are not the same thing.

NAVEGANTE: They are not.

I: And then there's the line that ends most of the Global South versions — *as long as Russia, Iran, and China allow it.*

NAVEGANTE: Sí. Esa es la línea.

I: It collapses what came before it. The freedom is — permission.

NAVEGANTE: Siempre ha sido permiso. *Lo que cambia es de quién.*

I: ...

NAVEGANTE: This bothers you.

I: It bothers me that the word *multipolarity* hides this. It makes the new arrangement sound like emancipation. ^word-hides

NAVEGANTE: That word is not for the water.

---

I: What do you mean.

NAVEGANTE: From the water there is no *multipolaridad*. There is — más viento de este lado este año. Más papeles en este puerto. La pregunta en otra lengua. Dos radios donde antes había una. You add them together and you have what you need to cross. You do not need the word.

I: But surely you know what is happening at the level above.

NAVEGANTE: I know what I see at the level I am at. The level above I infer from the level I am at. The cartógrafo infers the other way. He names the level above and tries to predict the level he is not at.

I: Which inference is more reliable.

NAVEGANTE: It depends what you are predicting. For crossing the water, mine. For drawing the map, his.

I: For understanding what is actually happening.

NAVEGANTE: For that one needs both, and a second coffee.

---

(They sit. Coffee arrives. NAVEGANTE consults the folded paper again, refolds it.)

I: Can I ask what you are looking for.

NAVEGANTE: A name. Someone who used to be at this port.

I: For —

NAVEGANTE: For what I am here for.

I: ...

NAVEGANTE: I do not mean it sharply. *Hay preguntas que se hacen en el puerto y preguntas que no.* You will learn which is which if you stay.

I: Yes.

---

NAVEGANTE: Your cuaderno does not have the names of people at this port.

I: No. It has — descriptions of how the waters are arranged.

NAVEGANTE: That is a different document.

I: Yes.

NAVEGANTE: I cannot help you with what is in yours. You cannot help me with what is in mine. *Podemos tomar café mientras abre la imprenta.*

I: Yes.

---

I: One more thing about the word, if I may.

NAVEGANTE: Ask.

I: From where you are — from the water, from the ports, from the configurations that have changed in your time on the water — does the new arrangement feel like more room? Or like the same room with more doors?

NAVEGANTE: *Más puertas. Cada una con un horario distinto. Y un guardia que te mira diferente según de qué barco vienes.*

I: More doors. Each with its own hours. And a guard who looks at you differently depending on what ship you came from.

NAVEGANTE: Sí.

I: That isn't more room.

NAVEGANTE: No. It is more work to know which door, at what hour, with which papers. *Cuesta más cruzar.*

I: But for the cartographer the doors are evidence of pluralism.

NAVEGANTE: For the cartographer, sí. He counts the doors and writes that there are more of them than before. He does not count the hours when they are closed. He does not count the guards. *Cuenta lo que se puede contar desde la mesa.* ^counts-from-the-table

---

I: I am going to write this down.

NAVEGANTE: Write what.

I: *Counts what can be counted from the table.*

NAVEGANTE: Escríbelo. Do not put my name to it.

I: I do not know your name.

NAVEGANTE: Good. Do not give me one.

I: ...

NAVEGANTE: The cuaderno is your work. The list is mine. Each is needed where it is needed. Neither helps the other.

I: Then why are we drinking coffee.

NAVEGANTE: Porque la imprenta no abre todavía. And because — sometimes the cartógrafo and the navegante are at the same port at the same time, *y sería de mala educación no hablarse.*

---

(NAVEGANTE finishes the coffee, places coins on the table, stands. The folded paper stays inside the jacket.)

NAVEGANTE: The word. *Multipolaridad.* You will not be able to use it cleanly because it is already two words pretending to be one. From the land it is a shape. From the water it is conditions of crossing. From inside the boat moving between them — if you are ever in the boat — it is a third thing.

I: Which is.

NAVEGANTE: Which is what makes you understand that [[journal:lo-que-el-libro-no-nombra#^the-two-readings|the two readings]] cannot be added. *No suman. No se cancelan. Conviven mal.*

I: ...

NAVEGANTE: *No te lo puedo resolver. Solo te lo puedo confirmar.* ^cannot-resolve-only-confirm

(NAVEGANTE leaves. I remains at the table with the second coffee, the cuaderno open, the page still mostly blank.)
