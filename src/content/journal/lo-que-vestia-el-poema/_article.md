I wrote Renata a poem structured as a loan. *(A devolver después de leer.)* Ten sections, each one a catalog of what the waiting was doing to me — the patient version running out, the soft version drying up, the version still capable of tenderness signing off with a date of expiry. The piece closes with the request returned to its envelope: *este poema no es tuyo. es un préstamo. devuélvemelo. como voy a tener que hacer con toda esta ternura si no te decides pronto.*

She never read it. I had blocked her, in the same week I wrote it.

A loan that cannot be repaid because the lender has closed the channel. A poem returned by no one because no one received it. The artifact is structurally complete — preface, body, signature, request — and structurally unable to do what it claims to do. It cannot return. The frame names a transaction that cannot occur. ^closed-loan

What was the poem actually for, then. That's the question this entry is.

---

Two monkeys, in a primatologist's enclosure, are play-fighting. One bites the other. The bitten monkey doesn't run, doesn't yelp, continues the fight. Bateson, watching this in the early 1950s, found the question hidden inside it: how does the monkey on the receiving end know the bite is not a bite? Considered as physical event, the bite is a bite. Teeth, skin, pressure. What converts it into not-a-bite is a meta-message — somewhere in the exchange, a signal that says *this is play*. Without the signal, the bitten monkey would have responded as if attacked. With it, the bite belongs to a different game.

Bateson called this the play frame. Over the next twenty years he generalized it ruthlessly. Therapy: the line *tell me about your father* is not the same outside the consulting room as inside. Theater: the actor saying *I love you* on stage is and isn't, and everyone in the room knows. Eulogies: things can be said over a body that, said in the parking lot afterward, would be insults or admissions. The frame doesn't suspend reality. It rearranges what counts as reality at this scale of communication. The metacommunicative signal — *this is play, this is therapy, this is theater, this is a eulogy* — is itself a message, and [[journal:smash-laterally-i#:%7E:text=The%20metacommunicative%20signal|it does the most work]]. This is [[journal:smash-laterally-i#^meadows-leverage|paradigm-level intervention]] in Meadows' taxonomy: not changing the parameters but shifting what counts as the game.

Poems are frames. *This is a poem* tells the reader how to receive what follows: attend to the language as language, weigh the figures, suspend literalism, hold the speaker as voice rather than person, allow the address to be sincere or ironic or both or neither. Not a small contract. It permits writing that, outside the frame, couldn't be written. It also permits *receiving* writing that, outside the frame, couldn't be received.

A poem framed as a loan addressed to someone who has been blocked is, considered as a piece of language, a contradiction in transactional terms. As a Bateson frame, it is something else. The frame is what allows the catalog to be catalogued at all.

---

Jakobson in 1960 gave us the most useful map of what *function* a piece of language can perform. There are six. The referential — talking about something. The emotive — expressing the speaker. The conative — acting on the addressee. The phatic — keeping the channel open. The metalingual — talking about language. And the poetic — orienting toward the message itself, foregrounding the form. Jakobson's actual claim, often misread, is that any utterance has all six functions running at once; what makes something *poetic* is that the poetic function becomes dominant. Not exclusive. Dominant.

The diagnostic question, then, for any of my own poems written into and out of the rupture with Renata: not whether the poetic function is present. It is. Which *other* function is riding underneath, and what does the poetic dominance do to it.

This is the work the conservatory drawer needs.

---

Three modes the frame permits. Each of them has a specimen.

The first is the mode where the frame permits content that, outside the frame, would constitute the act it describes. The loan-poem is this. Its content is leverage — a catalog of what the waiting is costing me, structured to register on her if she received it. As a text message, the same words are coercion. As a poem framed as a loan, with returns and interest, the words become something else: a record the speaker keeps of his own erosion, that *also* would have functioned as leverage if it had ever been sent. The frame doesn't dissolve the leverage. It separates the leverage from its delivery. The artifact got written; the threat-shaped content became visible to the writer; nothing arrived at the addressee. The frame did exactly the work of the bite that signals not-a-bite. The teeth were real. The bite did not happen.

This is the mode that's most easily mis-described as *cover*. Cover hides. The frame doesn't hide — it rearranges. The leverage is fully visible inside the frame, on the page; what's foreclosed is its operation as leverage. That foreclosure is also work the frame does.

Worth noting what the loan-poem was *not* responding to. The week I wrote it, the rupture had already had its full epistolary exchange. I had written first — saying I'd deleted her from my contacts, that the situation was hurting more than helping, that I couldn't keep waiting for her to read my stories or give me a minimum of attention, that I needed to think about my own self-preservation. I had proposed three months of distance with one last meeting before the pause. I had also written, near the close of that message, that if she didn't reply I would understand it was all for nothing and that I had wasted my time as so many times before — the same wasted-time framing the loan-poem would later spend ten sections elaborating. The threat-shape was already in the initiating letter. The loan-poem didn't invent it; it deepened it.

She had answered the next day, in a register I had not seen from her before. A measured paragraph, slightly formal. She had felt overwhelmed by the intensity at the party. She had been trying to explain on the balcony that she was not emotionally available. She did not want me to wait. Three months sounded reasonable. This was, she said, the affective responsibility she could provide. *Esta es la responsabilidad afectiva que te puedo proporcionar.* The phrase is specific to a Mexican millennial therapy-fluent register I recognized but had not heard her use toward me before. It read as a sincere closure. It also read, depending on which day I'm doing the reading, as a structurally elegant exit — agreeing to the form of distance I had proposed, in language that made refusal look ungenerous, with no meeting and no follow-up. Both readings remain available. The exchange is not the kind of artifact that resolves them.

What is unambiguous is that both letters had been written. The loan-poem came later, after the exchange had concluded, addressed to a figure that neither of those letters had quite described — the evitativa who had chosen the European, who had named me as *more than disposable* and treated me as less, who had dishonored a friendship I had been ready to adapt to. That figure may be accurate to the woman in some registers and wrong to her in others; from inside the closed loop the loan-poem belongs to, the question doesn't settle. What's clearer is that the loan-poem was less a response to the exchange than a continuation of the threat-shape my own initiating letter had already opened. The frame let me keep elaborating that shape for months after the actual exchange had stopped having anyone in it to receive it.

The second mode is harder to see. The frame permits content the writer hasn't yet figured out is what they're saying. I wrote a poem in the same season — a different one, addressed to a third woman whose voice was reaching me across the continent. The piece was about cartography, about a heart that wasn't a customs office, about the discovery that the map of desire could have more than one capital. I had not, in argument, arrived at any of those propositions. The poem arrived at them by way of meeting the formal demands of itself. Line-break and figure-choice and the slow extension of the central metaphor did what argument couldn't have done in the same time: they let the doctrine surface, in form, before I could have stated it as a thesis. Without the frame, the content didn't exist in a form I could sort. Reading it now, the doctrine is questionable — the heart-as-airport image was doing as much justification as discovery — but the doctrine *exists* to be questioned because the poem found it first. The frame was the channel.

The third mode is auto-suasive. The frame permits the writer to say to himself what he needs to hear, in a register that bypasses his own resistance. Two of the pieces in the drawer are exactly this — one declaring that the territory I'd built in five years would not be surrendered to anyone else's failure of nerve, the other naming a phantom kingdom of unclaimed offers and walking away from its court. The pep talk doesn't work in prose; it sounds like what it is. In line-break it works, because the writer is meanwhile occupied with figure-choices and isn't watching himself try to convince himself. The poem regulates the writer, often without his consent. He reads it back, days later, and finds something has been said into him.

The three modes are not exclusive. The loan-poem runs all three at once — leverage in front, self-witnessing underneath, auto-suasion in the corners where the catalog of erosion turns into a kind of dignity. The work isn't sorting poems into modes. It's reading the ratios.

The thing the figure-on-the-page was protecting me from looking at directly is something the prose-pass can name now.

The rupture wasn't a failure of character. Hers or mine. It was that two people kissed at the wrong relational moment for what the kisses turned out to have been. She had just ended two years with Oliver. Her emotional unavailability wasn't a refusal aimed at me; it was structural — the actual condition of someone who had recently left a long relationship and did not have, and was not obligated to have, the resources for what was opening. Saying *I am not emotionally available* was honest. It described a state, not a verdict. The misreading the figure-on-the-page sustains is the misreading where her unavailability gets translated into rejection. It was not rejection. It was a description of the conditions under which she could meet what was happening, and those conditions were that she could not yet meet it.

I wanted to meet her in the middle. At [[journal:al-borde-del-fenomeno|the edge of phenomena]] — to use the framing this journal has been building toward — where two systems converge and new differences emerge through their convergence. I was eager to adapt to her rhythm, her constraints, her timeline, because what the kisses had registered seemed to me to warrant adapting to. She did not have, in that moment, the symmetric capacity. The metacommunicative channel through which we would have negotiated what the kisses meant — what they could become given who and where each of us was — never got built. Without that channel, the kisses became unreadable. Without readability, the closure became asymmetric: her measured letter, my catalog of erosion, the block, the silence. The story is not who failed. The story is that two people who had registered each other did not have the conditions, the time, or the metacommunicative tools to build a configuration that could hold what had been registered.

The figure-on-the-page foreclosed this reading because this reading does not give the writer an enemy. It gives the writer a structural loss. Structural loss is harder to carry than betrayal. Betrayal at least produces blame, and blame at least produces motion. Structural loss produces nothing but the slow recognition that no one in the situation acted against the writer; the situation itself was unmeetable on the timeline it had.

There is a sharper layer underneath the structural loss, and it belongs in this article because the loan-poem cannot be fully sorted without it. The kisses did not only register Renata to me. They registered something to me about the almost-four years that had ended four years before her. Two kisses with a person whose own moment couldn't hold them did, retroactively, a kind of cybernetic work the almost-four years had not done at any point in their duration: they showed me, by being unlike, what those almost-four years had been. They had not been love. They had been a relationship sustained, with great skill, by someone who knew the right words to convince me to stay. My ex was a good therapist. I am saying that with care, not as accusation. She did know the right words. She did help me, repeatedly, in difficult situations. The care was real, the continuity was real, the building of a shared life was real. What had not been there was [[journal:lo-que-corrige-el-mapa#:%7E:text=The%20dog%20has%20read%20the%20relational%20pattern|the recognition the kisses produced]] — the registering at the depth I had been registering, the meeting at the rim of what each system could not have generated alone. I had mistaken being well-handled for being met. The kisses gave me the comparison data. After them, the previous almost-four years could not be re-described as anything other than skilled handling I had needed and accepted. That is a Bateson-level event: a single experience that reorganizes the meta-pattern of how the writer learns to be in relationship at all. *[[journal:lo-que-corrige-el-mapa#:%7E:text=the%20difference%20that%20makes%20a%20difference|Difference that makes a difference]]* is the technical phrase. The kisses were the difference. The almost-four years were what the difference made legible. ^kisses-as-difference

This is why the loss is disproportionate to the relational facts. The conventional reading — *it was just two kisses, get over it* — fails because it treats the wound at the level of what happened between us. The actual wound is at the level of what the kisses revealed about my own capacity to mistake handling for meeting, and at the level of the recognition-event itself, which was specific to the conditions that produced it and cannot be replicated by Renata's eventual return or by anyone else's arrival. She was, in the strict cybernetic sense, the perturbation that triggered the structural change. The instrument is gone. The data is permanent — [[journal:smash-laterally-ii#^parkinsons-bill|the damage that won him the fight was paying out in installments]], and so is this.

This also explains why the friend-circle silence was the silence it was. The available frame for what had happened was the romantic-triangle frame: *with her, or with him?* The rupture got translated into that grammar by people trying, in good faith, to organize the social field — [[journal:al-borde-del-fenomeno#^substrate-ownership|who owns the substrate determines what can be thought]], even at the scale of a social circle. But the romantic-triangle frame cannot carry a recognition-event. It cannot carry the almost-four-years-as-therapy reframe. It cannot carry the structural-timing loss. Inside the romantic-triangle frame, my continued grief became inexplicable — *she chose someone else, get over it* — because the frame did not have the categories the grief was actually about. [[journal:smash-laterally-i#:%7E:text=both%20visible%20options%20inside%20the%20frame%20punish%20you|I could not speak inside that frame]] without distorting my own data. So I did not speak. The silence in the circle wasn't only that they didn't reach out. It was that the language available was the wrong language for what had happened, and I did not yet have a different language to offer.

---

What is love. The question the rupture forced is not abstract. It is the very specific question of what the kisses had that the almost-four years did not. The diagnostic the article has been performing — the play frame, the three modes, the closed-loop, the figure-vs-letter — has been serving this question all along. The angry version of the loss let me delay arriving at it. The loan-poem gave me an enemy where the situation had only a structural loss. The verse-pass in *El Fin del Rodeo* got further toward the question by clearing the imagined audience out of the corner. The prose-pass arrives at the question itself.

The provisional answer the kisses gave is something like this. Love, in the cybernetic register Bateson and Pask and Maturana give us tools to think with, is [[journal:lo-que-corrige-el-mapa#:%7E:text=mind%20wasn't%20something%20inside%20a%20head|the configuration two autopoietic systems negotiate]] when neither absorbs the other and neither stays isolated — the recognition that happens at the edge where each system perceives that the other is also a system, also organizing itself, also incomplete in the same way each is incomplete. The almost-four years had had care, fit, history, mutual investment, the building of a shared life. What it had lacked was that edge-encounter. It had been one system being skillfully shaped around another that did not, or could not, see it back at the same depth. The kisses were the first time I had been seen at the depth I was trying to see. Two events. Then the channel through which the events occurred closed.

This is the question the open loop has been holding open. Not *what was the loan-poem doing*. That is the foreground, and the foreground is now sortable. The background, which the foreground was protecting me from looking at, is *what would it have taken to meet her at that edge given the timing we actually had*, and *what is the configuration I am now living toward, knowing the difference between handling and meeting, knowing which of those was almost-four years and which was two kisses*. Those questions have no answers I can extract from the page. They have answers I will or will not build into the next encounter, whoever it is with. ^what-is-love

---

Reading the ratios is itself a poetic act, and I have already performed it once.

In April I wrote a piece in five sections that put Renata in the corner of a dark room and told her the rodeo was over — the long detour through her imagined gaze, the public her absence had been performing, the witness the flowering still felt it needed. The piece named what the witness was for. It also named what came after the witness left: *quedo yo aún viviendo, redimido y resurrecto.* That was the first pass at the diagnostic this article is the second pass at. The first pass happened in verse, with all the affordances verse gives — rhythm, accumulation, the right to leave things unresolved. The second pass is in prose, with months of distance the verse didn't have. Both are real. The verse-pass got further into the affect than this one will. The prose-pass gets further into the structure. ^two-passes

This is also why the diagnostic doesn't close. The verse-pass produced an image — the empty corner where the imagined audience used to be — that the prose-pass can't improve on. The prose-pass produced a vocabulary — three modes, the play frame, the rarely-solo poetic function — that the verse-pass couldn't have. The corpus is read by both, and both readings are partial. The thing being read is not the kind of thing one reading completes.

---

What this asks of me, and of any writer doing this work, is not to police the frame. Policing the frame is the move where you decide a poem is *really* a threat or *really* a petition or *really* auto-suasion and dismiss the aesthetic dimension as cover. The move is wrong because, as the second mode showed, the frame is also the channel through which the content first became visible. Policing it forecloses the diagnostic. The writer who learns to dismiss every poem as the function-under-the-poem will never again use the form to find out what he is feeling. He will have lost the channel.

What it asks instead is to [[journal:lo-que-corrige-el-mapa#:%7E:text=calibration%20is%20just%20the%20long%20memory%20of%20correction|keep the channel open]] for sorting later. To read the poem six months on, with distance, and ask: what was that one doing. What was its dominant function under the aesthetic. Did the frame protect me from saying something I shouldn't have, or did it permit me to say something I shouldn't have. Was this a poem that did its work, or a poem that legitimated something that needed to be heard otherwise. The answer is sometimes humbling. It is occasionally embarrassing. Both can be true, and the both-can-be-true is what the frame was for.

---

The same shape, in a smaller register, as the closed loop a previous entry traced. A system that loses contact with its environment doesn't learn, it confabulates; the [[journal:lo-que-corrige-el-mapa#:%7E:text=the%20map%20starts%20to%20refer%20only%20to%20itself|map alone becomes self-referential, what looks like coherence is escalation]]. A poem alone in its frame, addressed to someone who will never read it, is exactly that closed loop. The form survives by being challenged. By being read by something that is not it. Including, eventually, by the writer himself, when enough time has passed that he is not it either.

It has been one hundred and fourty-one days since the rupture and fifteen since I told two friends the part of it I had been carrying alone. The thesis is at 97.5% — last revisions in form, not in content. The loan-poem is still in the drawer. The diagnostic doesn't close. What it does is get longer loops, with more correction in them.

That's most of the work. Not dismissing the poems. Staying in conversation with them long enough to find out what each one was doing — including the one she was never going to read.

The remainder, which the diagnostic was never going to handle by itself, is the part the article has only now arrived at: that there is no one to blame and nothing left to want from her — [[journal:smash-laterally-iii#^inside-empire|you stand inside the empire, not outside]], and so do I inside this loss. Not because the wound is small. Because the wound is at a level the loan-poem's vocabulary couldn't reach. She had just left two years. I had been ready, against the actual chronology, to meet her in the middle. The metacommunicative tools we needed to negotiate what the kisses had been about were not available to either of us in time. That is structural loss, and structural loss does not have a villain to forgive, only a configuration to grieve. I have not, across one hundred and fourty-one days, found a way to hate her. I no longer expect to. What the kisses gave me — the recognition, the comparison data, the difference that made the four years legible as something other than what I had been calling them — is permanent. The instrument is gone. The data stays. For that data, given [[journal:smash-laterally-ii#:%7E:text=lateral%20moves%20are%20wagers|the cost at which it arrived]] and the gift it nevertheless was, what survives across all the sorting and across the silence and across the loop the loan-poem belongs to is something that does not sit naturally inside any of the article's analytic frames, and that the analytic frames have been preparing me to be able to say without it being one more iteration of the loop. Thank you.

The question that data opened — *what is love* — I will keep open. It is the question the next encounter, whoever it is with, will or will not let me build a different answer to — [[journal:smash-laterally-iii#^handoff-to-reader|the next person is the reader, also inside the system]]. The diagnostic served the question. The question outlasts the diagnostic.
