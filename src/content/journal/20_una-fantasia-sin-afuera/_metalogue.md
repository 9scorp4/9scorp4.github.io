(A second-floor apartment on rue de Lanaudière, late spring. The window is open. JÉRÉMIE has invited the usual mix — two people from the press, a visitor from Berlin, EL INVERSOR who is here through someone who could not come, and I. Six at the table, seven counting JÉRÉMIE moving between the kitchen and the sideboard. Someone outside is calling a dog.)

(JÉRÉMIE has switched to English because EL INVERSOR does not speak French. *Jérémie ha cambiado de idioma sin que nadie se lo pida. Yo también. Es la primera cosa que noto y la primera cosa que no digo.*)

---

(The conversation has been about the press for a while now. The visitor from Berlin asks about a book the press published last year. JÉRÉMIE answers with what sounds like real fondness for the book.)

EL INVERSOR: How many copies of that one moved, in the end?

JÉRÉMIE: Six hundred and fifty.

EL INVERSOR: And the unit cost?

JÉRÉMIE: I'd have to check.

EL INVERSOR: But broadly — you're not making money on these.

JÉRÉMIE: No. We're not trying to.

EL INVERSOR: Mm.

(The *mm* is the first thing I notice, except it is not what I think it is. EL INVERSOR is not dismissing the press. He is translating it. *Seiscientos cincuenta entró en su cabeza como un número que significa una cosa; el libro entró en la vida de Jérémie como una cosa que significa otra. La traducción es invisible porque los dos están hablando con números.*) ^numero-que-significa

(JÉRÉMIE refills wine. The conversation forks. The Berlin visitor asks EL INVERSOR what he is working on now. He says he is between things. He says it in a way that suggests *between things* is a serious phase, not a pause.)

---

EL INVERSOR: And what about you?

I: I write. Some research, some analytical work.

EL INVERSOR: What kind of research?

I: Anthropology. I'm finishing a master's.

EL INVERSOR: On what?

I: Systemic patterns. The way certain kinds of harm get distributed across populations through ordinary institutions.

EL INVERSOR: So policy work.

I: ...

I: Not exactly. It's more descriptive than that. Tracing what something does, in practice. Through people who don't know they're producing the pattern.

EL INVERSOR: Right. So advocacy.

I: ...

I: It's neither, really. The argument is about how the institutions are organized.

EL INVERSOR: Sure. And what's the deliverable?

(The word *deliverable* enters the room like a chair being moved. JÉRÉMIE has come back to the table; I see him register it. He does not say anything.)

I: A thesis. Then probably some articles.

EL INVERSOR: For?

I: For peer review, mostly. Other anthropologists.

EL INVERSOR: How does that scale?

(*La pregunta no es de mala fe.* It is a real question, asked seriously, by someone who has spent his life solving versions of it. The question asks how the work, having been done, propagates. The seat believes propagation is the form of consequence.)

I: It doesn't, exactly. It does something else.

EL INVERSOR: What's the something else?

I: The work participates in a conversation. The conversation is slow. It changes what people in adjacent fields can think.

EL INVERSOR: Got it. So influence.

I: ...

I: Maybe. The word feels off.

(EL INVERSOR is patient. He is not trying to dismiss what I am saying. He is trying to find the handle on it — the variable name he can attach to what I do so that he can think about it. *Y eso es lo que está pasando. Está buscando el nombre de la variable.*) ^nombre-de-la-variable

(JÉRÉMIE comes back to fill wine. The look across the table happens here. Briefly. He sees it. He moves on, because the dinner is the dinner.)

EL INVERSOR: Okay, let me try this differently. If your work were five times faster, what could you do with the extra time?

I: ...

I: I don't think the work has a speed in that sense.

EL INVERSOR: Everything has a speed.

(He says it warmly. As a kind of correction someone gives a younger person whose framing is missing something.)

I: The slowness might be part of what it is.

EL INVERSOR: Slowness as a feature.

I: ...

I: Yes.

EL INVERSOR: That's interesting. There's a guy I know — he's doing something similar in qualitative research. Says the bottleneck is reading time. He's training a model on the corpus.

(*No es una respuesta. Es lo que mi respuesta significó cuando entró al asiento donde él está sentado. La lentitud se convirtió en bottleneck. La lectura se convirtió en ingestion. Lo que dije no fue refutado. Fue traducido. Y traducido, ya no era lo que dije.*) ^no-fue-refutado-fue-traducido

I: ...

I: That sounds like a different thing.

EL INVERSOR: Maybe. But adjacent.

JÉRÉMIE: (from the kitchen) Salade?

(The conversation forks again. The Berlin visitor turns to ask JÉRÉMIE something. EL INVERSOR turns to the person on his other side. The exchange is over. It lasted perhaps four minutes.)

---

(The dinner continues. Wine is refilled. Someone tells a story about a trip they took last winter. EL INVERSOR laughs at the right places. JÉRÉMIE serves the salad. The Berlin visitor describes a building in Friedrichshain that has been turned into something none of the previous things it was. EL INVERSOR asks two follow-up questions that are both interesting.)

(I am eating the salad.)

(*El asiento no me oyó. No me oyó porque no me podía oír. No es que no quisiera; es que el asiento — el de él y el del mundo que lo sostiene — está construido de tal forma que lo que dije no podía entrar sin ser traducido a otro idioma. Y traducido, ya no era lo que dije. Era otra cosa que tiene mi nombre encima.*)

(JÉRÉMIE comes back to the table. He sits beside me for a moment. He says, low, *ça va?*)

I: Ça va. The salad is good.

JÉRÉMIE: He's not a bad guy.

I: I know.

(That is the moment. JÉRÉMIE knows. He has not intervened. He cannot intervene. Doing so would be making a scene, and the scene is the encounter itself, which has already happened and has already ended without anyone being able to name what it was.) ^la-cena-es-la-escena

---

(The dinner ends around eleven. EL INVERSOR takes a car. The Berlin visitor is staying with JÉRÉMIE. The two people from the press leave together, walking. I walk home along Bélanger. It is warm enough to be pleasant. *Nada me ha pasado. Solo cené.*)

(*Pero algo me pasó. Y lo que me pasó es lo que el artículo trata de nombrar. La diferencia entre lo que dije y lo que el asiento oyó es la diferencia que el artículo trata de cargar. No fue un mal encuentro. Fue exactamente el encuentro que el asiento permite que ocurra. Por eso es difícil de describir. Porque salió bien.*)

(I reach the apartment. I do not write any of this down. I put away the keys. I drink a glass of water. Tomorrow there will be other things.)

(*Pero antes de dormir, lo que sí me queda: la cena fue cordial. La cena fue, en su superficie, sin daño. Y lo que se perdió en la traducción no le pertenece a él. Le pertenece a quienquiera que haya pensado que la cordialidad y la traducción no eran lo mismo.*) ^cordialidad-y-traduccion
