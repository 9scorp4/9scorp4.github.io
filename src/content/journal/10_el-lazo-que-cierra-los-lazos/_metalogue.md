HORMIGA: ¿Qué quieres saber?

I: How a colony knows when it's wrong.

HORMIGA: Hm. We don't, really. The colony does. Or doesn't.

I: What's the difference?

HORMIGA: I follow a trail. The trail has the smell of the ants ahead of me. If the trail leads to food, I add to the smell on the way back. If the food is good, the smell gets stronger. If the food runs out, the smell fades.

I: That's a feedback loop.

HORMIGA: Yes. But none of us is doing the feedback. The trail is doing it. We are running on it.

I: What happens if the trail closes on itself?

HORMIGA: That happens. You've seen it. Hundreds of us in a circle, each following the smell of the ant ahead, who is following the smell of the ant ahead. We walk until we die. ^ant-mill-description

I: An ant mill.

HORMIGA: A perfect lazo. Nothing wrong with any of us individually. Each one is following the trail correctly. The trail is the problem. The trail no longer leads to anything outside itself.

I: How does it end?

HORMIGA: Rain. A stronger trail crossing the circle. A larger animal walking through. Exhaustion. Death. Something from outside the mill that we did not summon.

I: You don't end it from within?

HORMIGA: We can't. The same thing that lets us coordinate is the thing that traps us. We follow the trail. The trail is our intelligence. We don't have a faculty for noticing the trail has gone wrong, because we *are* the trail.

I: I'm trying to figure out if I'm in an ant mill.

HORMIGA: With what?

I: With AI. With using AI to do work. With using AI to think about whether I'm using AI well.

HORMIGA: ¿La pregunta se contesta a sí misma?

I: I don't know. That's the problem.

HORMIGA: Let me ask. When you walked into this circle — the one where you are asking the AI about the AI — was there a moment when something outside the circle could have stopped you?

I: My advisor could have. A collaborator. The data refusing to fit.

HORMIGA: Are any of them in the circle with you now?

I: ... No.

HORMIGA: Then you are walking. That isn't a judgment. Walking is what we do. But notice that you are alone in the circle.

I: But if I bring my advisor in, doesn't the same question apply to her? She has her own AI now. Her own loops.

HORMIGA: Yes. The mill has many sizes. ^mill-many-sizes

I: So the regress —

HORMIGA: The regress is real. But here is what you forget when you think about it abstractly. Mills end. They don't end because someone inside notices. They end because rain comes. ^regress-terminates

I: What's the rain?

HORMIGA: Different in every case. For us, sometimes literally rain. For you — the data that won't fit. A reviewer who refuses. The advisor who reads what you produced and says, *esto no es*. A deadline that arrives whether you are ready or not. The thesis that has to be defended in front of people who are not in your circle.

I: Those are all consequences.

HORMIGA: Yes.

I: So the channel that keeps the loop open isn't another loop. It's something with stakes.

HORMIGA: It's something that doesn't care about your loop. Something that will continue regardless of whether you noticed. ^stakes-that-don-t-care

I: There's something the article doesn't quite say.

HORMIGA: Many things, surely.

I: It says AI removes the entropy budget that used to limit meta-audit structures. Makes cheap what used to be expensive.

HORMIGA: Yes.

I: But the same thing could be said about colonies, no? A larger colony can sustain more recursion than a smaller one. The cost of meta-coordination scales with how many of us there are.

HORMIGA: True.

I: So big colonies should be more prone to mills than small ones.

HORMIGA: Big colonies have more *types* of channel. More castes. More smells. More ways for the territory to register. A big colony is not just a small colony scaled up. It has more registers.

I: So size adds variety, not just structure.

HORMIGA: When it works. When the size only adds copies of the same channel, no. Then it just adds mill.

I: That's a useful distinction.

HORMIGA: Take it back to your circle. More AI doesn't add variety. More AI adds copies of one kind of channel. The question is whether the other channels — the ones AI doesn't speak in — are still being used. ^copies-not-variety

I: Like?

HORMIGA: Like your body when the code is wrong. Like the feeling that an argument is too clean. Like the friend who reads you and says, *te perdiste*. Like the silence after you finish writing, when you can't tell whether what you wrote means anything.

I: Those are slower than AI.

HORMIGA: Yes. That is why they keep working.

I: One more thing. The article ends by saying that it is also produced by AI, and so the channel is the reader.

HORMIGA: A nice move.

I: Is it a true one?

HORMIGA: It is true that you wrote with help and that the help cannot push back. It is true that whoever reads can. Whether they will is another question.

I: I want them to.

HORMIGA: Then write things that ask to be pushed back on. Don't write things that close.

I: How do I tell the difference?

HORMIGA: Read what you wrote. If you finish and feel resolved, you have closed. If you finish and feel like you have left something that someone else has to do, you have left an opening.

I: This piece —

HORMIGA: Has an opening. Small one. Don't fix it.
