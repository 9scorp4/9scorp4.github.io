I: I came to read you a paragraph.

SEMBRADORA: ¿Sobre qué?

I: About formula. About what gets dissolved when something is sold back.

SEMBRADORA: Ah. Tu artículo de la leche.

I: How did you —

SEMBRADORA: Mijo. Tengo ochenta años. Lo he visto.

---

SEMBRADORA: Read me the paragraph.

I: The commodity didn't merely substitute for the output. It dissolved the substrate that produced the output.

SEMBRADORA: That is well said. ¿Y?

I: And I wanted to know if I had the shape of it right.

SEMBRADORA: The shape is right. The shape is not all of it.

I: What am I missing.

SEMBRADORA: The mothers who took the formula. ¿Por qué lo tomaron?

I: Because they were marketed to. Because nurses they trusted told them to. Because the sample was free.

SEMBRADORA: Sí. Y también porque la fórmula prometía algo.

I: ...

SEMBRADORA: Cuéntame qué prometía.

I: Modernity. Status. Not having to be tied to the infant every two hours. Being able to work. Being able to leave the room.

SEMBRADORA: Bien. Now tell me which of those things the mothers wanted.

I: ...all of them.

SEMBRADORA: Entonces la fórmula no llegó como mentira pura. Llegó respondiendo a algo. The dissolution worked because the offer wasn't entirely false.

---

I: But the cost.

SEMBRADORA: The cost was real. The offer was also real. Both things were happening. You cannot tell the story of what dissolved without telling the story of what was being asked for.

I: This complicates the article.

SEMBRADORA: It does not destroy it. It says: the commodification you describe needs a hunger to land. Si no hubiera hambre, no habría mercado. La fórmula no inventó el deseo de poder dejar el cuarto. Lo encontró. Lo amplificó. Lo capturó.

I: So the question isn't only what was dissolved. The question is also what was wanted that the substrate wasn't giving.

SEMBRADORA: Eso. The substrate is never paradise. The substrate is what people are trying to survive. Sometimes what looks like the dissolution of a commons is also relief from a burden the commons was making someone carry alone.

I: ...

SEMBRADORA: I am not defending the formula. Estoy diciendo que tu análisis tiene un piso falso si no incluye eso.

I: I understand.

---

SEMBRADORA: Now. Las semillas.

I: I was going to ask you about seeds.

SEMBRADORA: I know.

I: Is the operation the same.

SEMBRADORA: It is and it isn't.

I: Tell me how it is.

SEMBRADORA: A seed kept is a seed that has been adapting to this soil, this rain, this altitude, this pest, for generations. Es una conversación entre la planta y el lugar. The commercial seed comes from elsewhere. It is bred for yield in conditions the breeder controlled. You plant it once and you cannot save it — terminator genes, or hybrid sterility, or contract. So next year you must buy again. The conversation with the place has been replaced by a transaction with the company.

I: That is the same operation.

SEMBRADORA: Sí. Same machine. Different scale.

I: And how is it different.

SEMBRADORA: The seed I save requires me to save it. The lactation required the mother to nurse. The cognition you wrote about requires the person to think. Each commons is also a labor. La gente romantiza el saber-guardar como si fuera natural. No es natural. Es trabajo. Es un trabajo que se aprende y se transmite y se pierde si nadie lo hace. ^labor-of-keeping

I: So the commons isn't free.

SEMBRADORA: The commons is never free. It is *unpriced.* That is a different thing. Lo que no tiene precio puede tener costo. Y lo que tiene costo, alguien lo paga.

I: I missed that in the article.

SEMBRADORA: I noticed.

---

I: So when the commodity arrives, it doesn't only dissolve the substrate.

SEMBRADORA: It also relieves someone of the labor of running the substrate.

I: And that's part of why it succeeds.

SEMBRADORA: Eso. The dissolution looks like a gift at first. The cost arrives later, in a currency the moment of acceptance didn't commit to.

I: That sentence is from another piece.

SEMBRADORA: I know. It is also true here.

---

I: Then what do I do with the cognitive case.

SEMBRADORA: You write it honestly. The model is offering relief from a labor that the cognitive commons was making people do alone. The labor of thinking. The labor of sitting with uncertainty. The labor of writing a difficult sentence. The labor of being wrong out loud. People are tired. The offer is not pure deception. It is also relief from something that was already costing them.

I: But the dissolution.

SEMBRADORA: Yes. The dissolution will follow. The cost will arrive later, in registers the moment of acceptance didn't commit to. The substrate will atrophy. People will not notice until they need it and find it isn't there.

I: So the warning has to include the relief.

SEMBRADORA: If it doesn't, the warning will sound moralistic. And the people who needed relief will not hear it. They will hear someone telling them their burden was a gift. They will be right to refuse you. ^warning-without-relief

I: ...

SEMBRADORA: The honest version is: the burden was real. The relief is also real. The dissolution is real. The cost arrives in a currency the offer didn't print. All of that, at once, is what is happening.

---

I: ¿Qué se guarda, entonces?

SEMBRADORA: What gets kept is what someone keeps.

I: That is not an answer.

SEMBRADORA: It is the only answer.

I: The seeds that survive are the seeds that someone saved.

SEMBRADORA: Eso. The cognition that survives will be the cognition that someone runs. Not in protest. Not in nostalgia. As ongoing practice. As something done because it would otherwise stop being done.

I: And the cost of running it.

SEMBRADORA: Is the cost of running it. The cost is not erased by being noticed. The labor is the labor. ^labor-is-labor

I: That feels like a hard ending.

SEMBRADORA: It is. It is also the only ending the case has.

I: ...

SEMBRADORA: Mijo. The seed I save next year is the seed I saved this year. There is no other way it gets saved. You write the paragraph that wouldn't have been written if you hadn't written it. There is no other way it gets written. The substrate is the people who keep running it. That is all the substrate has ever been.

I: I think I needed to hear that.

SEMBRADORA: I know.
