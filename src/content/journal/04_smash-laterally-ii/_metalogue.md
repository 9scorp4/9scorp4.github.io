I: I want to ask you about Kinshasa.

ALI: That is not the first lateral.

I: I know.

ALI: Then why did you start there.

I: Because it's the famous one. The visible one.

ALI: It was also the second one. Why are you asking it first.

I: Because the first one is harder to talk about.

ALI: That is the answer. Now ask the question that is actually behind it.

I: How do I know if a lateral move is going to be worth it.

ALI: You don't.

CASSIUS: You don't, before. You don't, after.

I: ...Who are you.

ALI: The man I would have been if I had not refused.

CASSIUS: The man you would have admired more comfortably.

I: ...

ALI: Yes. He is going to talk too. There are only two of us, this time.

---

ALI: You wrote about Kinshasa.

I: Yes.

ALI: You wrote it well enough. You missed the part inside the body.

I: I tried to put it in.

ALI: You wrote about the punches landing. You did not write about what the punches did to the parts of me that were not in the contest.

I: The brain.

ALI: I had a brain before that fight. I had a brain after it. They were not the same brain. The fight was eight rounds. The difference between the brains was a slow leak that took ten more years to show.

I: I'm sorry.

ALI: Don't be sorry. I knew what I was doing. Approximately. The point is that the fight ended in October. The bill did not. The bill came in installments for the next three decades, and most of the installments were not in any currency the fight had asked me to commit to.

I: That's the deferred cost.

ALI: It is also what happens when you defer a cost to a part of yourself that has not been told it is going to pay.

---

CASSIUS: I had the same brain.

I: The same body.

CASSIUS: Not the same career. I would have fought differently. I would have stayed inside the punch-trade model. I would have lost more fights to Foreman, but I would have lost them in three rounds, not eight. Less head trauma per fight. More fights in total. My career would have been longer and shallower.

I: Would you have been champion?

CASSIUS: Probably not at the level he was. I would have been champion of something. The world has many belts.

I: Would you have lived longer?

CASSIUS: Statistically, no. Boxing kills its champions at roughly the same rate either way. The shape of the dying changes. I would likely have died of cardiovascular disease, like most retired heavyweights. He died of Parkinson's complications. Different ways. Comparable ages.

I: ...

CASSIUS: This is the part you didn't expect to hear. You wanted me to be the version that was healthy and old. I am not. I am the version where the bill came in a different envelope.

I: Esperaba que tú fueras la versión que no pagaba.

CASSIUS: I know. It is the same thing he is showing you. There is no version where the bill does not come.

---

I: I want to ask about the first one.

ALI: 1967.

I: Yes.

ALI: I refused induction. I lost my title. I was banned from boxing in every state for three and a half years. I lost the prime years of an athlete's career.

I: That cost was visible.

ALI: That cost was visible to me. It was not visible to most of the people who admired the refusal. They admired it as a moral act. They did not picture the years.

I: ...

CASSIUS: I had those years.

I: What did you do with them.

CASSIUS: I fought. I stayed inside the title for as long as I could hold it. I made money. I bought houses. The footage exists in the parallel where it does. Nobody made a documentary about it.

I: Was it the same body?

CASSIUS: It was a body that had been hit fewer times. So no. By 1970, my brain was already different from his.

I: Better different?

CASSIUS: Less damaged. Not the same as better. I did not have the moral career either. I had the boxing record and the bank account and the brain that was less leaky in 1970, and I did not have the cultural figure he became. There are no clean trades. ^parallel-cost

I: ...

ALI: He is right. Both of us paid. The shape of the bill differs. The fact of the bill does not.

---

I: How do I know if a lateral move is going to be worth it.

ALI: You don't.

CASSIUS: You don't, before. You don't, after.

I: I asked that already.

ALI: The answer is the same.

I: ...What do I do then.

ALI: You make the move you are going to make and you find out what you have decided.

CASSIUS: Sometimes years later.

ALI: The decision is in the body before it is in the choice. By the time you find yourself deciding, the deciding has already started without you.

I: Then where is the lateral move actually made.

ALI: Earlier than you think. In the slow shift of who you are turning into.

CASSIUS: The visible refusal is the part that becomes legible.

ALI: The earlier work is in becoming a person who can refuse. ^becoming-dialogue

I: Then I cannot plan it.

ALI: No.

CASSIUS: You can only notice that you have already started.

I: Then who do I find next.

ALI: Whoever has more time before the bill arrives.

CASSIUS: They always pay too. Just later.
